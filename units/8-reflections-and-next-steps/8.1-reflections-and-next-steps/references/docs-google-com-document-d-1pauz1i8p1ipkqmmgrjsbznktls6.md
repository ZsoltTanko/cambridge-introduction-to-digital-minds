# Reflections and next steps - Brainstorming document

**Linked from:** Exercise 1 of Unit 8. The exercise asks learners to make a copy of this brainstorming template and use it to reflect on the course and draft a short four-minute presentation for their group.

## What it is

A Google Doc template, to be copied by each learner, that structures the end-of-course reflection. It has no separate author byline beyond the BlueDot course context. It is a working document rather than a reading: the learner fills in their own answers in their own copy.

## Key content

The template runs in three steps.

**Step 1: Review.** Learners spend about 10 minutes going over their own course notes and their group's discussion docs, then at least 15 minutes writing responses to a set of prompts. The prompts are grouped under four headings: remember and understand (overall reaction, what inspired or frustrated them, whether their view changed), analyse (topics that felt undertreated, the balance of over- and under-attributing moral patienthood, arguments that the field's assumptions may be wrong, which questions look most tractable), evaluate (where they stand on working on digital minds, and their core concern about risks or opportunity costs), and create (skills they bring, a real-world problem they would like to solve, a tool they wish existed, connecting two course concepts, explaining a key topic to a novice). The template says the goal is to generate ideas, not to refine them yet. It also points to the research agendas in the unit's supplementary readings for inspiration.

**Step 2: Brainstorm your presentation type.** Learners pick two or three contribution types from a list: a concern or open question, an argument for or against working on digital minds, a critique of a field assumption, an explainer on an undertreated topic, an original idea or framework, a policy or governance proposal, a project idea, a personal career reflection, or something else.

**Step 3: Prepare the presentation.** The template asks for a one-sentence topic headline, a feedback request for peers, two to three sentences on why it matters, three or four key points, a closing of one to three sentences, a note on whether to share supporting material, and a timed read-aloud to check it fits in four minutes.

## Why it's linked here

The exercise text says to make a copy of this doc and work through the prompts. The link is the working tool for the exercise, so the learner needs it at the point where the instruction is given.
