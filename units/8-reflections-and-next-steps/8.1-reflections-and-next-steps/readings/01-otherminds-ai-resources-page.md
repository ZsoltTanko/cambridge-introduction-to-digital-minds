# Otherminds.ai Resources Page

**One-line takeaway:** This is a curated directory of organisations, fellowships, grants, courses, events and newsletters in AI welfare, sentience and digital minds, and the course asks you to use it as a map of who is working in the field and how to get involved.

## Where this sits in the course

**Section context.** Section 8.1 (Reflections and Next Steps) is the first half of the final unit. Its page says the unit is for consolidating what you have learnt and preparing a contribution to your group. It lists three questions: what the main organisations in digital minds work are and how you might get involved, what you are taking away from the course, and what 4-minute contribution you want to share. Its learning goals are to identify relevant organisations and institutions, examine current research and find gaps, analyse principles for research on digital minds, and articulate your own thinking. The main work is in the brainstorming doc (Exercise 1), which asks you to reflect on the course and turn one idea into a short presentation.

**What the course guide says.** The guide asks you to browse the site to get a sense of the organisations and opportunities in the field, and to note the organisations active in digital minds and other relevant opportunities and resources. In short, it is a map of who is doing what.

**Role in the argument.** This is the only reading in the section that is a directory rather than an argument. Its job is practical: it is the raw material for the page's first question ("what are the main organisations ... and how might you get involved?"). It is also the most direct input to Exercise 1, because a 4-minute group contribution about next steps needs to name real institutions and real entry points, such as fellowships and grants. It is rated Core because the course treats knowing the landscape as a learning outcome, not as background.

**Priority and unit question.** Core. The unit's question is what you can do next after the course. This page answers "where" and "how" at a shallow level. The other readings in the section answer "on what principles" and "what remains unknown."

## Purpose of the reading

The page is a resource index. It is maintained by the Otherminds.ai team and presents itself as a hub for AI welfare, sentience and cognition research. It is aimed at someone who has finished the course and wants to know which organisations, programmes and events they could join, apply to, fund or learn from. It does not argue for a position on digital minds. Its implicit claim is that the field is now large enough to have an ecosystem worth navigating.

The page answers one question: which actors and opportunities exist in this area right now? It does not answer whether they are good, how to apply, or whether the field is well-founded.

## Detailed summary

The page header reports the following tab counts: Organizations (10), Fellowships (5), Grants (3), Courses (1), Events (6) and Newsletters (1). The source file reproduces each tab. Each entry has a name, a short blurb and a set of topic tags. Only the organisations carry a URL in the source file; the other tabs list no links. The tags come from a small vocabulary: AI welfare, Moral status, Consciousness, AI cognition, Philosophy, Safety, Alignment, Interpretability, AI rights and AI emotions.

### Organizations (10)

- **Eleos AI Research**: a nonprofit focused on the wellbeing and moral patienthood of AI systems.
- **NYU Center for Mind Ethics and Policy (CMEP)**: an academic centre studying the moral status of nonhuman minds, including AI and invertebrates. The blurb says it is led by Jeff Sebo.
- **PRISM (Partnership for Research Into Sentient Machines)**: a UK nonprofit investigating sentient machines.
- **California Institute for Machine Consciousness (CIMC)**: described as a new 2025 institute on machine consciousness, including work by Joscha Bach on consciousness hypotheses.
- **Conscium**: a research organisation promoting responsible approaches to conscious AI.
- **Sentient Futures**: a field-building organisation that runs courses, project incubators, residencies and conferences, with a focus on sentient beings including artificial minds and animals.
- **Rethink Priorities**: a research nonprofit with a worldview investigations team that refines models of digital consciousness and moral weight.
- **Anthropic Model Welfare Program**: an internal research team, led by Kyle Fish, that evaluates the welfare and moral status of Claude models.
- **Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence (LCFI)**: a Cambridge centre whose Consciousness and Intelligence programme studies the emergence of consciousness in AI systems.
- **Center for the Future of AI Mind & Society (FAU)**: organises MindFest, which focuses on sentience, autonomy and human-AI interaction.

### Fellowships (5)

- **Future Impact Group (FIG)**: runs research fellowships with projects on AI sentience, welfare and digital minds.
- **Anthropic Fellows Program**: a 4-month paid fellowship with a dedicated Model Welfare workstream, also tagged for interpretability and alignment.
- **Pivotal Research Fellowship**: a 9-week, London-based fellowship covering AI safety, including welfare topics.
- **ERA Fellowship (Cambridge)**: a summer fellowship in technical and governance AI safety, with options for digital minds projects.
- **MATS (ML Alignment & Theory Scholars)**: a research training programme. Some scholars work on welfare-adjacent topics such as interpretability and model cognition. Welfare is not its core focus.

### Grants (3)

- **Longview Philanthropy: Digital Sentience Fund**: funds research and public education on consciousness, sentience and the moral status of AI.
- **Coefficient Giving**: described as supporting digital minds work as a focus area that is expected to expand in coming years.
- **Survival and Flourishing Fund (SFF)**: supports several AI welfare and digital minds organisations.

### Courses (1)

- **Digital Minds Course**: described as an 8-week online course on the perceptions and plausibility of digital minds and their implications for ethics and society. This is the course you are taking. The listing's "8-week" figure differs from the course's own structure of 8 units, whose unit pages estimate roughly 2 hours each. The listing may describe the course's calendar, not its study time.

### Events (6)

- **NYU Mind Ethics and Policy Summit**: on the consciousness, sentience and moral and legal status of nonhumans, including AI and invertebrates.
- **Sentient Futures Summit**: conferences in San Francisco and London that bring together digital minds, animal welfare and AI safety communities.
- **Conference on AI Consciousness and Welfare**: organised by Eleos AI Research, focused on evaluating AI sentience.
- **MindFest (FAU)**: an annual event on machine consciousness, sentience and human-AI interaction.
- **AISB Convention (University of Sussex)**: the Society for AI and Simulation of Behaviour convention, with Anil Seth as keynote. Digital minds topics are welcome.
- **ICCS AI and Sentience Conference**: an International Center for Consciousness Studies event with talks by Chalmers, Blackmore and Hulme on AI consciousness.

### Newsletters (1)

- **Digital Minds Newsletter**: written by Will Millership, Bradford Saad and Lucius Caviola, covering AI welfare, sentience and digital minds research.

### What the page does not contain

The page gives no application deadlines, no fellowship start dates, no grant sizes or eligibility rules (except that the Anthropic fellowship is described as paid), no event dates, and no contact details beyond the links. It also does not say how the list is curated or how often it is updated. Everything in the summary above is paraphrased from the page, and none of it has been checked against the organisations' own sites.

## Techniques & methods explained

*Replaced for this org/program page with "What you'd do and learn" (see below).*

### What you'd do and learn

The page is not a method, but it implies a workflow for a learner who wants to get involved. A practical reading of the page supports four steps.

1. **Map the landscape.** Use the tag system to sort entries by relevance. "AI welfare" and "Moral status" pick out the organisations focused on welfare. "Consciousness" and "AI cognition" pick out the more theoretical or cognitive-science work. "Safety" and "Alignment" pick out the entries where digital minds work sits inside AI safety.
2. **Pick an entry point by background.** The page implies different routes for different profiles. Research-track people can look at the fellowships (for example FIG, Pivotal, ERA and MATS), field-builders can look at Sentient Futures, and funders or grant-seekers can look at Longview, Coefficient Giving and SFF.
3. **Track events and newsletters.** The events and the newsletter are the cheapest ways to keep current. The newsletter is the only item on the page that is an ongoing information stream.
4. **Verify before acting.** Because the page gives no dates or deadlines, a learner should go to each organisation's own page before applying or planning. Treat the blurbs as a starting point.

What you would learn from this is a vocabulary of institutions and the boundaries between them. In practice, the page shows that digital minds work is spread across academic centres, nonprofits, a company-internal team, fellowships that are mostly AI safety programmes, and grant-makers.

## Fit & trade-offs

**Strengths of the directory as the source presents it.** It is broad (organisations, fellowships, grants, courses, events and newsletters in one place). Its tags let a reader filter quickly. It names several entry routes that are not obvious: the Anthropic Fellows Program, the Sentient Futures Summit, and the Digital Sentience Fund.

**Limits the reading itself shows.**

| Issue | What the page shows | Why it matters for you |
|---|---|---|
| No dates or deadlines | Fellowships and events have durations or locations but no application windows or dates | You must check each source before planning |
| Blurbs are short | One or two sentences per entry | Enough to orient, not enough to judge fit |
| Tags are coarse | Ten tags cover the field | Entries tagged "AI welfare" can differ a lot in their research |
| Mixed programme types | Internal company teams, academic centres, nonprofits and safety training programmes sit side by side | Access, independence and funding models differ and the page does not say so |
| Welfare is not always central | MATS and ERA are described as having welfare options, not welfare focus | Check the programme's actual research agenda before applying |
| Possible staleness | Directory of a fast-moving field, with a 2026 date and no update history | Entries may have closed, moved or been renamed |

**Points for you to weigh (my analysis, not the source's).** The directory has no visible inclusion criteria, so its coverage reflects one curator's choices. Some listed actors are internal to AI companies, which can raise questions about independence, and the page does not discuss that. The list is also almost entirely in the Anglophone and Western academic and philanthropic space, which may not represent the wider field.

## Key concepts & terminology

- **Digital minds** - the course's umbrella term for AI or other artificial systems that might have minds, consciousness or morally relevant states. The page uses it throughout.
- **AI welfare** - study of whether AI systems can be harmed or benefited, and how to take that into account. A tag on the page.
- **Moral status / moral patienthood** - whether a being is owed moral consideration in its own right. Used in several entries.
- **Consciousness (machine consciousness)** - the question of whether and how AI systems have subjective experience. Tagged in many entries.
- **AI cognition** - the study of what AI systems represent, reason about and understand. Tagged separately from consciousness.
- **Model Welfare** - the programme name used by Anthropic for its internal team and fellowship workstream that evaluates welfare of Claude models.
- **Fellowship** - a time-limited, usually funded research or training programme. Here it covers both paid residencies and summer or short programmes.
- **Grant-maker** - a funder of research or organisations. The page lists three.
- **Field-building** - activity that grows a research area through training, community, events and infrastructure. Used for Sentient Futures.
- **Worldview investigations** - the name of Rethink Priorities' team that, per the page, refines models of digital consciousness and moral weight. The page does not define the term further.

## Critical perspective

*This section is my own analysis, not the source's view.*

- **Curation without criteria.** The page does not say how entries were chosen. A directory like this shapes what newcomers see, so the absence of inclusion rules is a meaningful gap.
- **Mixed incentives.** Several listed actors are funders, AI companies, or organisations that depend on the field's growth (Sentient Futures calls itself field-building). That does not make their work weaker, but a reader should know who is funding what.
- **Staleness.** The page is dated 2026 and the field is moving fast. Entries like the CIMC (new in 2025) and the Anthropic Model Welfare Program may change scope. Check current status before relying on any entry.
- **Naming changes.** The list includes "Coefficient Giving" as a grant-maker, and I believe this is the renamed Open Philanthropy. I am not certain of that from the source alone, so verify it before citing it.
- **Gaps.** The directory has no entries from outside the Anglophone and Western academic and philanthropic space. It also has few governance-focused organisations and no entries on animal or invertebrate welfare apart from the NYU centre and Sentient Futures.
- **Other perspectives.** Readers who take a more sceptical view of AI moral status, such as those who think current systems lack the features needed for welfare, would argue that a directory which takes the field's existence for granted should say more about its open questions. The course's own readings on contested consciousness theories would support that pushback.
