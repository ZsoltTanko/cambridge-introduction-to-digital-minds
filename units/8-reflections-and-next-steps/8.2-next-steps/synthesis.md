# 8.2 Next steps - Synthesis
*Unit 8: Reflections and Next Steps | Time not stated (page is a short closing list) | 0 core / 0 further readings (6 linked resources profiled below)*

Note: this page has no assigned readings, exercises or reference notes. The synthesis is built from the page text and the captured source text of each linked resource. Source snapshots were captured around August to October 2026, and most application windows had already closed by then. Treat dates and deadlines as a snapshot, not as current.

## What this section is teaching

This is a signposting page, not a teaching page. It answers one question: after the course, how do I keep learning about digital minds and how do I start contributing? It lists six routes:

- stay informed (the newsletter),
- go deeper with a structured programme (the Cambridge Digital Minds Fellowship),
- find the wider ecosystem (the Beginner's Guide events list),
- do research under mentorship (Future Impact Group),
- enter through adjacent AI safety pipelines (MATS),
- run a project of your own with a mentor (Sentient Futures).

It sits at the very end of the course arc. Units 1 to 7 built the conceptual and practical toolkit: moral standing, theories of consciousness, evaluation, public perceptions, safety interactions, decisions under uncertainty and governance. [8.1](../8.1-reflections-and-next-steps/page.md) consolidates this, surveys organisations and research principles, and asks for a 4-minute contribution. 8.2 turns that consolidation into concrete next actions.

## The core argument

The through-line of the page, and of the linked sources, is that digital minds is a nascent field with few established paths, so you often have to build your own entry route.

The fellowship page states this plainly: unlike AI safety and governance, digital minds research is "still nascent", so "identifying promising research questions is harder, career paths are less clear, and the community is smaller." It also admits there are "fewer established positions" in the area. That framing explains why the page offers a portfolio of options rather than a single career ladder. Many of the options are not even labelled digital minds. They are adjacent programmes (AI safety fellowships, effective-altruism-adjacent incubators) that happen to host digital-minds mentors and projects.

The options fall into three layers.

**Staying current.** The [Cambridge Digital Minds Newsletter](../../../sources/cambridge-digital-minds-newsletter__0302c018.md) is a quarterly round-up with over 1,000 subscribers. Its archive shows four issues so far, from a December 2025 year-in-review to a September 2026 issue. The page adds a practical hook: the fellowship tells you to sign up to the newsletter to hear when applications open. The newsletter is therefore both a learning resource and the notification channel for the course's own pipeline.

**Structured entry.** The [Cambridge Digital Minds Fellowship](../../../sources/cambridge-digital-minds-fellowship__410d87af.md) is the direct continuation of this course. The 2026 edition was a five-day residential programme at Jesus College (3 to 9 August), followed by a two-day Strategy Workshop. It took 15 fellows, was fully funded with a £1,000 stipend, and was run with Rethink Priorities and PRISM. Its three modules map neatly onto the course. The first is philosophical and technical foundations (LLM architectures, consciousness and moral status frameworks). The second is societal implications and strategy (scenario planning to roughly 2100, intervention design). The third is project scoping and career planning. Completing the online course, or showing equivalent familiarity, is an explicit prerequisite, and mentors are paired at least a month ahead. Its stated target audience is graduate students, postdocs and early-career academics (especially in philosophy), professionals in government, policy or industry who want to pivot, and exceptional earlier-stage individuals. Its stated skill is project scoping, which it treats as the hard part of a young field.

**Ecosystem and project-based routes.** The [Beginner's Guide events list](../../../sources/beginners-guide-to-digital-minds__728d3db7.md) is the map. In the captured snapshot (37 events, updated 25 August 2026) it listed seminars, conferences (Eleos, ASSC, Models of Consciousness), jobs and grants, as well as the fellowships below. The three named programmes differ in what they ask of you:

- [Future Impact Group](../../../sources/future-impact-group__0e9cd3bb.md) offers part-time (8+ hours a week) mentored research projects. The AI Sentience stream is led by figures such as Robert Long and Rosie Campbell (Eleos), Jeff Sebo, Patrick Butlin, Derek Shiller, Brad Saad, Kyle Fish, Janet Pauketat and Seth Lazar. Projects include research ethics for AI welfare, better welfare evals, a Code of Practice for digital sentience research, individuating digital minds, beliefs about AI sentience and normative competence evaluations. It asks for specific skills: ML engineering, social science, bioethics, law, or well-read generalists with strong writing.
- [MATS](../../../sources/mats__fb0dee66.md) is a 12-week, fully funded AI safety research fellowship (Berkeley and London) with an optional 6 to 12 month extension. It is not a digital minds programme. Its streams span alignment, interpretability, security and governance, though the events list notes that digital-minds-relevant mentors have appeared in past cohorts, including Eleos researchers in the Autumn 2026 cohort. It suits people with technical profiles who want the strongest established pipeline into AI research, with digital minds as an angle.
- [Sentient Futures Project Incubator](../../../sources/sentient-futures__987c894e.md) is a 10-week remote programme (31 August to 9 November 2026) at 5 or more hours a week. It pairs you with one of 80 or more mentors to deliver a standalone output. Its scope is broader than AI: animal welfare, artificial minds and AI governance, united by widening the moral circle. You can join a mentor's pre-defined project or propose your own.

Across these routes, several shared patterns appear. Almost every route is mentor-based. Time commitments range from a weekend (a research sprint listed in the guide) through 5 to 8 hours a week to full-time fellowships. Many are free or funded. Most are cohort-based with deadlines, and in the captured snapshot nearly all of the named cohorts were closed, so the practical advice is to track the next round via the newsletter and the events list rather than to expect an open application.

The framing also echoes the course's own themes. Sentient Futures explicitly selects for people who "act under uncertainty" and "widen the moral circle", which restates the Unit 6 posture and the Unit 1 question of who counts. The FIG project list is effectively a map of open problems from earlier units: self-report-based welfare evaluations and their limits (Unit 3), beliefs about AI sentience (Unit 4), research ethics and governance (Unit 7), and individuation of digital minds (Unit 2 and 3 themes). Reading the project descriptions is a good way to find a research gap, which is exactly what 8.1 asks you to do.

## Techniques & approaches compared

This section is not about techniques, so the table compares the paths.

| Path | How it works | Strengths | Weaknesses | Trade-offs / open problems |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Newsletter | Quarterly digest of news and research | Zero commitment; broad overview; tells you when programmes open | Passive; no skill-building or network | Low cost, low leverage on its own |
| Cambridge Digital Minds Fellowship | 7-day residential programme plus workshop; mentor pairing; 15 fellows | Direct continuation of the course; strong network; project scoping; funded | Very selective; one short cohort a year; 2026 round closed | Highest density, lowest availability |
| Beginner's Guide events list | Curated list of events, jobs, grants | Wide coverage; includes talks, conferences, sprints (some no expertise needed) | Time-sensitive; listing is not endorsement or vetting | Needs regular checking; many items are adjacent (consciousness science, AI safety) |
| Future Impact Group | Part-time (8+ hrs/wk) mentored projects with named leads | Concrete research questions; leads are well-known; builds a portfolio | Wants specific skills (ML, social science, law); limited technical support on some projects; cohort-based | Research output depends on your autonomy |
| MATS | 12-week funded full-time fellowship; extension possible | Prestigious, well-resourced pipeline into AI research | Not about digital minds; competitive; requires full-time relocation | Choose it for capability and credibility, then steer toward welfare questions |
| Sentient Futures Incubator | 10-week remote, 5+ hrs/wk, mentor of your choice (up to three applications) | Accessible and flexible; own-project option; includes AI and animal welfare | Broader than digital minds; output is a single piece of work | Breadth helps moral-circle thinking but dilutes focus |

Open problems the page itself surfaces: there are few paid, permanent positions in the field; entry depends on being in the right cohort window; and the best mix of philosophical versus technical skills is unsettled.

## Tensions, debates & open questions

- **Dedicated versus adjacent routes.** For dedicated routes: the Cambridge fellowship and FIG's AI Sentience stream focus on the topic and put you among the right people. For adjacent routes: MATS and similar programmes have far more established infrastructure, funding and career outcomes, and the events list shows digital-minds mentors appearing there. The strongest case for adjacency is that jobs and credibility currently sit in AI safety. The strongest case for dedication is that adjacent programmes may leave the digital-minds question as a side project.
- **Technical versus philosophical skills.** FIG and Eleos-linked projects ask for ML engineering and eval-building. The Cambridge fellowship leans toward philosophers and policy people. The field arguably needs both, and the page does not resolve which investment pays off most.
- **Breadth versus focus.** Sentient Futures groups artificial minds with farmed fish, insects and wild animals under one moral-circle banner, which gives conceptual coherence but may spread effort. The events list also flags that the California Institute for Machine Consciousness is interested in engineering conscious systems rather than welfare, a reminder that "AI consciousness" communities do not share goals.
- **Is the field ready to absorb newcomers?** The fellowship admits positions are scarce and says it aims to be "realistic about career paths". Encouraging entry into a small field risks oversupply of aspirants; the counter is that the field is explicitly capacity-constrained and short of people able to scope projects.
- **Snapshot staleness.** Almost all listed cohorts were closed when the pages were captured. The page therefore points to a pipeline, not to open doors, which makes monitoring channels (newsletter, events list) the real action.

## Study paths

**30-minute path (essentials).**
1. Skim the page text (5 min).
2. Read the [Fellowship page](../../../sources/cambridge-digital-minds-fellowship__410d87af.md) for the field framing and the three modules (10 min).
3. Skim the [events list](../../../sources/beginners-guide-to-digital-minds__728d3db7.md) for 3 items that match you (10 min).
4. Subscribe to the newsletter (the [archive](../../../sources/cambridge-digital-minds-newsletter__0302c018.md) shows what to expect).

**Full path (about 1.5 hours).** Add the [FIG](../../../sources/future-impact-group__0e9cd3bb.md) project list (identify two projects you could contribute to and the skills they ask for), the [Sentient Futures](../../../sources/sentient-futures__987c894e.md) programme page, and the [MATS](../../../sources/mats__fb0dee66.md) overview. Then write a half-page plan: one learning habit, one programme to apply to next cycle, one small project.

**Deep dive.** Read the full events list including the "recently closed" items to see what recurs each year. Read each FIG project's skill requirements in detail. Use the 8.1 further readings ([Research priorities for AI welfare](../8.1-reflections-and-next-steps/readings/04-research-priorities-for-ai-welfare.md), [Research Agenda: Social Science of Digital Minds](../8.1-reflections-and-next-steps/readings/03-research-agenda-social-science-of-digital-minds.md), [Digital minds governance: early scoping](../8.1-reflections-and-next-steps/readings/06-digital-minds-governance-early-scoping-from-expert-interview.md)) to pick a question, then match it to a mentor.

## Discussion questions

1. Recall: which of the six listed options are dedicated to digital minds, and which are adjacent?
2. Recall: what does the Cambridge fellowship expect of applicants before they arrive?
3. Why does the fellowship make project scoping a core goal? What is harder about scoping in a nascent field than in AI safety?
4. What do the three mentored programmes (FIG, MATS, Sentient Futures) have in common, and how do they differ in time commitment and focus?
5. Which FIG project corresponds most closely to a debate or limitation you met earlier in the course, and why?
6. Make the best case for entering digital minds through MATS rather than a dedicated programme. Then make the best case against.
7. The fellowship admits there are few established positions. How should that affect how much you invest in the field, and in what form?
8. Sentient Futures groups artificial minds with animal welfare. What does that framing gain and lose?
9. Given that most listed cohorts were closed in the snapshot, what is a sensible plan for the next 12 months?
10. Which skills (philosophy, ML, social science, law, operations) do you have, and which listed route fits? Which gap would you close first?
11. Is it appropriate to steer newcomers into a field with unclear career paths? What obligations do programme organisers have?
12. Which idea from your 8.1 brainstorm would you take to a mentor, and what output would you promise in ten weeks?
