# Cambridge Digital Minds

**Linked from:** The introductory bullet points on the Unit 1 page, which say the course was developed by Cambridge Digital Minds and is hosted on BlueDot Impact's site. The link identifies the organisation behind the course materials.

## What it is

Cambridge Digital Minds is a research initiative at the University of Cambridge. It is based with the Centre for the Future of Intelligence (CFI) and works on how society should prepare for digital minds, meaning AI systems that may have, or may be perceived to have, minds with experiences and welfare that deserve moral consideration. Its stated mission covers consciousness, welfare, moral status, and how people should interact with such systems. The team listed on the homepage includes Dr Lucius Caviola as Director and Dr Henry Shevlin as Senior Researcher and Associate Director of CFI.

## Key content

The homepage frames the problem this way: experts disagree about whether AI systems could have morally relevant minds, and public belief in such minds may spread regardless of the truth. The organisation's work is split into three areas.

- **Research** aims to anticipate how societies will respond to potential digital minds and to develop governance strategies.
- **Capacity building** runs fellowships, training programmes and this online course, which the page describes as anchored in academic rigour.
- **Engagement** involves coordinating experts and working with policymakers, industry and the public to reduce confusion and polarisation before high-stakes conflicts arise.

The homepage lists recent publications. A few of the most relevant to this unit:

- A survey of 582 AI researchers and 838 nationally representative US adults on subjective experience in AI. The median estimate for such systems existing by 2100 was 70% among researchers and 60% among the public. Both groups saw safeguards as needed now, though support for welfare protections was far below that for animals or the environment.
- "Denying Personhood to AI," an analysis of 23 US state bills since 2022 that seek to deny legal personhood to AI systems. The authors argue these bills may be premature.
- "Moral Concern for AI," which introduces the AI Harm Game. In that study, 498 US participants harmed a GPT-4o-based agent for a small bonus in 1.4 of 3 rounds on average, despite most doubting that AI can suffer.
- Papers on how society will respond to AI consciousness, including an argument that disagreement, not convergence, is the likely near-term outcome.

Other sections cover the organisation's Digital Minds Fellowship, a strategy workshop, its newsletter on digital minds news, and a team of researchers. The homepage also advertises the 8-week Introduction to Digital Minds course, which is the course this unit belongs to. The snapshot says applications for the Autumn/Winter 2026 cohort were open until 13 September 2026, which had already passed when the page was captured.
