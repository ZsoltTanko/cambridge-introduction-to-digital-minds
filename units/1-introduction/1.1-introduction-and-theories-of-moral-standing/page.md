## Page text

**Welcome to Unit 1 of Introduction to Digital Minds!** We hope you get a lot out of this course and, out of this first unit. 

Some high-level points before we get started: 
- This course was developed by the team at **[Cambridge Digital Minds](https://digitalminds.cam/)**, and is hosted on BlueDot Impact's site.
- Please do the **core readings** and **written prompts** for each unit before joining your discussion session. **Optional** readings are not required, but are useful if you have time and want to go deeper on the topics discussed in the core readings.  
- Core readings are often **excerpts** of major papers - **please take note of the sections to read.**  The overall papers are worth reading if you have time, but the time to do this is **not** included in the 2 hours of preparation per week. 
- Use the **written prompts** as suits you - they're designed to support your learning, and rough notes are fine.

This is the **first of 8 units.** We will discuss this content in the week commencing 19th October. This unit will introduce questions like:
- What are we talking about when we talk about digital minds? 
- Might digital minds matter morally, and why might this question be worth considering now?
- Why might we worry about over- and under-attribution of moral standing? 

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:
- Define key terms and concepts relating to consciousness, sentience and moral standing
- Identify digital systems that might matter morally, or might be perceived as such
- List key routes to moral standing
- Identify concerns around potential or perceived moral patienthood of AI models
- Describe transhumanism, successionism, their critiques, and their potential relation to digital minds questions
- Describe reasons for and against prioritising work on digital minds
- Describe centrism concerning digital minds

**We hope you enjoy this unit!** Please add any feedback inline, and reach out to online-course@digitalminds.cam with any questions.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Written Exercise

*Free text · Core*

Choose **one** of the features below. 
    1. Consciousness
    2. Sentience
    3. Robust Agency
    4. Precarity

Do you think it is 1) _necessary_ and/or 2) _sufficient_ for moral patienthood? Why? 

Spend **5 minutes** noting down your response.

### Exercise 2: Written Exercise

*Free text · Core*

Consider the features in the prompt above _(consciousness, sentience, robust agency, precarity)_. 

Spend **up to 10 minutes** noting down your thoughts on how likely it is that the systems below might have these features a) now and b) in the future: 
    1. Foundation Models
    2. AI agents
    3. Whole Brain Emulations
    4. Neuromorphic computing
    5. Biological-computer hybrid systems.
