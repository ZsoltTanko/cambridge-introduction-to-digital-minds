# MIT AI Risk Repository: Domain Taxonomy of AI Risks

**Linked from:** Exercise 1 of section 5.1. Learners are asked to note three AI safety or ethics risks that concern them, and the exercise points to the "Domain Taxonomy of AI Risks" summary table as a source of inspiration.

## What it is

The MIT AI Risk Initiative (MIT FutureTech) runs a living database of AI risks, drawn from frameworks and classifications, together with governance and incident tools. The link lands on the "Domain Taxonomy of AI Risks" section, which classifies risks from AI into domains and subdomains. The taxonomy is one of three parts of the repository, alongside the risk database and a causal taxonomy. Data is licensed under CC BY 4.0.

## Key content

The taxonomy has 7 domains and 24 subdomains:

1. **Discrimination & Toxicity**: unfair discrimination, exposure to toxic content, unequal performance across groups.
2. **Privacy & Security**: leaking or inferring sensitive information; security vulnerabilities and attacks on AI systems.
3. **Misinformation**: false or misleading output; pollution of the information ecosystem and loss of shared reality.
4. **Malicious Actors**: large-scale disinformation, surveillance and influence; cyberattacks, weapons and mass harm; fraud, scams and manipulation.
5. **Human-Computer Interaction**: overreliance and unsafe use; loss of human agency and autonomy.
6. **Socioeconomic & Environmental**: power centralisation; inequality and job quality; devaluation of human labour; competitive racing; governance failure; environmental harm.
7. **AI System Safety, Failures & Limitations**: misaligned goals; dangerous capabilities; lack of robustness; lack of transparency; AI welfare and rights; multi-agent risks.

The domain most relevant to digital minds is domain 7, which includes a subdomain on AI welfare and rights. That subdomain is the only place where the taxonomy directly names the moral status of AI systems, which is the topic the exercise's welfare questions address.

The homepage itself is a hub. It links to a risk repository of over 1,700 risks from 65 frameworks, an AI incident tracker, a governance map of laws and policies, and an expert survey of 272 researchers on risk priorities. The page gives inconsistent counts: the hero text says 1,700+ risks and 74 frameworks, while the FAQ says 1,600+ risks and 65 documents.

Source note: the cached homepage did not contain this section, so the content above comes from a WebFetch of the repository's /risks page. That summary was produced by a small model and was not checked line by line against the page. The taxonomy's subdomain names may differ slightly from the live wording.

## Why it's linked here

The exercise asks learners to pick three risks that concern them, and this table gives a ready list of categories to choose from. It supports the exercise's next step, which asks how AI welfare ranks against those risks, because the taxonomy places welfare alongside other safety and governance risks. Learners can use the table to test whether they are thinking about risks in a structured way, not just by recall.
