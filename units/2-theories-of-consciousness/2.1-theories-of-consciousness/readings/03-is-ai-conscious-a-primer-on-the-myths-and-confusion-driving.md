The question the primer answers, as the course uses it, is roughly: what conceptual confusions must be cleared up before a claim that an AI is conscious can be assessed?

## Key concepts & terminology

Terms the course uses in this section (definitions from the course context, not verified against the paper):

- **Hard problem of consciousness** - why physical processes give rise to subjective experience at all; named on the section page.
- **Problem of other minds** - the difficulty of knowing whether others have experiences; named on the section page.
- **Gaming problem** - the worry that a system can be built to produce behaviour that mimics consciousness indicators; named on the section page.
- **Computational functionalism** - the view that mental states are individuated by their computational role, so that implementing the right computation suffices for mind; the section asks learners to explain and assess it.
- **Non-physicalist theories** - accounts on which consciousness is not exhausted by physical facts; flagged in the guide as part of the assigned pages.
- **Phenomenal vs. functional consciousness** - a distinction listed in the PhilPapers roadmap; its exact use in the paper is unverified.
