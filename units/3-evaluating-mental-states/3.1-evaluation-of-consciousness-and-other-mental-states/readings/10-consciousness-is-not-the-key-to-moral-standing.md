## Purpose of the reading

Papineau is a philosopher of mind. The chapter's title states its thesis. It is written for readers who accept that moral standing tracks consciousness, the view the chapter contests. The question it addresses is whether consciousness is the right property for deciding which animals and AI systems count morally, given that consciousness itself may not have sharp boundaries.

Per the course guide, this is an argument about indeterminacy: if consciousness is vague, a criterion built on it inherits that vagueness.

## Key concepts & terminology

- **Moral standing / moral patienthood** - whether a being's interests and welfare matter morally for its own sake.
- **Indeterminacy / vagueness** - a property has no sharp boundary, so some cases are borderline. The guide's central claim is that consciousness is indeterminate in this sense.
- **Borderline cases** - creatures or systems for which it is unclear whether a property holds. The chapter's critique depends on them.
- **Decoupling** (secondhand) - treating the question of moral standing separately from the question of consciousness.
