Unit 7 Glossary
The below terms may be useful in Unit 7: 




Governance, Digital Minds: policies, regulations, and institutional frameworks designed to manage the risks and ethical questions arising from AI systems that are - or are perceived as - conscious or morally significant. It crosses over with broader AI governance, and encompasses questions about how societies should respond to uncertainty about AI moral status.

Personhood, Legal. Key part of Western law, allowing a person, corporation or other entity to engage in the legal system as the subject of specific rights and duties. So, giving something legal rights and duties means making it a legal person. Broadly speaking, there are two types of legal personhood: 
* (i) Natural personhood - an inalienable personhood (we hold this as humans).
* (ii) Juridical personhood (also called "artificial" or "fictional" personhood) - corporations hold this. Unlike natural personhood, juridical personhood can be granted and dissolved by legal processes. 
This concept has been in use since Roman times, with a wide variety of entities covered from ships to corporations to rivers. While humans are naturally considered legal persons with a greater range of rights, purely legal persons have different standings - for example, corporations can own property but cannot marry.

Rights, Negative/Positive: rights are entitlements (not) to perform certain actions, or (not) to be in certain states; or entitlements that others (not) perform certain actions or (not) be in certain states. For example, negative rights might include duties of non-interference such as rights not to be harmed or destroyed, and not to be deceived or manipulated. On the other hand, positive rights (duties of provision) might include the right to maintenance, continued existence, access to information, or the conditions for flourishing.
