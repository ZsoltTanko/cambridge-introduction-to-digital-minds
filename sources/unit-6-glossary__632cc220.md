Unit 6 Glossary
The below terms may be useful in Unit 6: 



Precautionary Principle: the principle that when there is a threat of significant harm, scientific uncertainty should not be used as a reason to delay or refrain from undertaking proportionate measures to protect against that harm. Various formulations of this principle exist, and may make different recommendations for actions at different levels of uncertainty. One relevant approach is the EU’s, which recommends additional measures (non-discrimination, cost-benefit analysis, proportionality, consistency and an attempt to build more scientific understanding) for policies premised on precautionary thinking.

Suffering-risks: risks of astronomical (future) suffering or “s-risks” for short. For more discussion, see the Center for Reducing Suffering here. As relates to digital minds, the ease of copying and deploying digital systems - plus the potential for them to have different time perceptions or subjective experiences - leads to concerns that the scale of potential suffering could be extreme, should systems be able to suffer.

Uncertainty, descriptive: uncertainty about matters of fact, when we don’t know what is true of the world. This may be contingent (resolvable in principle with better evidence or theory) or intractable (resistant to resolution due to fundamental limits on what can be known). In the AI consciousness context, both contingent and intractable uncertainty may apply: we lack adequate theories and tests, and may face the deeper problem that subjective experience is not straightforwardly accessible to third-person scientific methods.

Uncertainty, moral: when we don’t know what is ethically right, or which ethical values, principles, or frameworks should guide action. It can arise even when the relevant non-moral facts are known.
