Introduction
________________
This week, you’ll recap the course, gather your thoughts, and prepare a short exploratory contribution to share with your group.
Your 4-minute presentation can cover anything: a project idea, open question, concern, critical perspective, career reflection – or something else entirely. The goal is informal and personal, not polished or definitive.
Use this doc to work through your thinking in three steps: review, brainstorm different types of contribution and prepare your presentation.
Step 1: Review
________________
1. Spend 10 minutes glancing over your own personal notes and your group’s discussion docs.
2. Spend at least 15 minutes noting down your thoughts in response to the prompts below. 
   1. You can use this however feels most useful - answer each prompt multiple times, skip some prompts, add more prompts - whatever works best for you. 
   2. Focus on generating ideas - we’ll refine them later.

Remember & understand — recall the key ideas and your first reactions
   * What is your overall response to reviewing the course materials?
   *    * Did any parts particularly inspire or frustrate you? Which, and why?
   *    * Did the course change your viewpoint at all? If so, how?
   * Analyse — interrogate the ideas and themes we discussed during the course
   * What topics felt missed or undertreated that you'd like to explore further?
   *    * Consider risks of over- and under-attribution of moral patienthood. Which concerns you more, and how might they interact?
   *    * What arguments can you make for why approaches or assumptions in the field may be mistaken, incomplete, or risky?
   *    * Which questions about digital minds appear most tractable?
   * Evaluate — reflect on where you stand and how your perspective has changed
   * Do you have a strong point of view on questions the course covered — routes to moral standing, AI consciousness, interactions with AI safety, governance?
   *    * Where do you sit on reasons to / not to work on digital minds, after the course?
   *    * Do you have a core concern about risks, opportunity costs, or unintended consequences of this work?
   * Create — make it your own
   * What unique skills or knowledge do you bring that could apply to these questions?
   *    * What's a real-world problem related to the course you'd love to solve?
   *    * What tool, website, or app related to these topics do you wish existed?
   *    * Connect two seemingly unrelated concepts from the course. What emerges?
   *    * Imagine explaining a key topic to a friend who knows nothing about it. What creative approach would you take?
   * You’ll carry forward your responses to some of these questions in the next section where you’ll begin to sketch out the ideas you want to discuss in your presentation. 
	

If you are looking for more inspiration, you might also consider one of the research agendas linked in the supplementary readings for this unit.
