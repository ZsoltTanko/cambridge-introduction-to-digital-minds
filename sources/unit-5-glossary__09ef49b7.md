Unit 5 Glossary
The below terms may be useful in Unit 5: 




AI Alignment: making AI systems do what we intend them to do (also referred to as intent alignment). An AI system is considered to be aligned if it advances its intended objectives. An AI system is misaligned if it pursues objectives that are not intended and at odds with its intended objectives. AI Alignment also refers to the goal of designing artificial intelligence systems in such a way that their objectives and behavior are aligned with the values and goals of human users or society at large. 

AI Ethics: a field that works to reduce current and near-term risks to humans associated with AI. These include harmful content, privacy violations, mental health impacts, and biased models already leading to discrimination and real-world harms. Note: the boundaries between AI Safety and AI Ethics are blurred, with areas of interaction and support and an overall shared objective of making AI safe for humans. At the same time, there are areas of tension, different emphases on particular risks are correlated with different groups.

AI Safety: a broadly technical field that considers whether and how AI development can be made safe for humans, particularly in terms of avoiding catastrophic (or even existential) harms. Actors within this field have different emphases. Some worries include superintelligent models aiding the concentration of power and the lock-in of a bad governing system; models pursuing unintended objectives (without us being able to stop them) or being misused by bad actors in ways that exacerbate risks from – or create impacts similar to – chemical, biological and nuclear weapons. Note: the boundaries between AI Safety and AI Ethics are blurred, with areas of interaction and support and an overall shared objective making AI go well for humans. At the same time, different emphases on particular risks are correlated with different groups.

AI Welfare: the potential wellbeing of AI systems; or the field of AI welfare, which studies whether AI systems can have interests, experiences, or preferences that matter morally – and if so, how (and whether) we ought to protect or promote them. Distinct but related to the field of AI safety, which considers whether and how AI development can be safe for humans.
